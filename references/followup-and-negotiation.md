# Follow-ups, Thank-yous & Offer Negotiation

Two short, high-leverage moments that come *after* the application and interview, and
share the kit's rules: written in the user's voice (`voice.md` +
`references/writing-standards.md`), grounded only in what's true (`fact-pack.md` and what
the user actually says), delivered copy-paste-ready, and **never auto-sent**. Both feed
the tracker (`references/application-tracker.md`) and the living library.

These are offered, not forced. Surface them at the natural moment — a thank-you right
after a debrief, a status nudge when the tracker shows an application gone quiet, a
negotiation brief when the user reports an offer.

---

## 1. The post-interview thank-you

Send within ~24 hours of an interview. Its job isn't etiquette theater — it's one more
honest, specific touch that reinforces fit.

- **Specific, not generic.** Reference something real from *that conversation* — a
  problem the interviewer described, a point of genuine agreement, a question you've kept
  thinking about. "Thank you for your time" with nothing concrete is worse than nothing.
  Pull the specifics from the debrief transcript if there is one; otherwise ask the user
  for the one thing that stuck with them.
- **Short.** 3–5 sentences. Warm, in their voice, no résumé recap.
- **Optional repair.** If the debrief flagged an answer that landed weak, a thank-you is
  a clean place to add the one sentence they wish they'd said — honestly, briefly, not as
  a do-over essay.
- **Per interviewer.** A panel gets a lightly distinct note each (same coordinated-
  evidence logic as cover letter + answers — don't send four identical notes).
- Deliver copy-paste-ready; the human sends. Log it on the role's tracker record.

## 2. The status-check follow-up (the polite nudge)

When the tracker shows an application or interview gone quiet past the normal window
(~7–10 days, or past a date the recruiter named), offer a brief, low-pressure nudge:
restate continued interest, reference the specific role, ask about timeline. One nudge,
not a campaign — and never needy. If a recruiter gave a date, wait for it first. Mark the
follow-up on the tracker so it isn't repeated.

---

## 3. Offer negotiation brief

When the user reports an offer, help them think it through and respond well. This is
advisory and truth-bound: **never fabricate a competing offer, a deadline, or a number**
the user doesn't actually have — manufactured leverage is the same lie as a fabricated
résumé, and it collapses just as badly.

**Build a short brief, not a script to read robotically:**

1. **Read the whole offer, not just base.** Base, bonus, equity (and what it's plausibly
   worth — note honestly when that's unknowable), benefits, start date, level/title.
2. **Anchor to their real numbers.** Pull `compensation.target`/`floor` from
   `profile.json`. Where the offer sits relative to both is the spine of the brief. If
   they have genuine market data (their own past comp, a real range from the recruiter, a
   real other offer), use it; never invent one.
3. **Find the honest levers.** What can truthfully move — base vs. a sign-on, level,
   start date, remote flexibility, a review timeline — and which the user actually cares
   about. Don't push them to negotiate things they don't want.
4. **Draft the response in their voice** — appreciative, clear, specific: enthusiasm for
   the role, the one or two asks framed around fit and the value they bring (from
   `fact-pack.md`), never an ultimatum unless they truly have and mean one. Give the
   email/verbal version they'll actually be comfortable saying.
5. **Know the walk-away.** Their `floor` is the honest line; name it plainly so they
   negotiate from a real position, and respect it if they say a number is fine.

Deliver as a brief + a draftable message. The user decides and the user sends. Capture
any durable new fact (their real floor moved, a market number they confirmed) to the
library per `library_enrichment.mode`, and update the tracker (`status: "offer"`, then
the outcome).
