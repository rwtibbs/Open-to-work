# Direct Outreach Playbook

The reference for **D. OUTREACH**. After an application is in, a personal note to the
right human can pull it out of the pile — but only when the fit is genuine. This
governs *how* to find the person, reach them, and write the note. As everywhere in
the kit, the user's `voice.md` and `fact-pack.md` govern *what* it says and *whose
voice* it's in; nothing here is person-specific.

The goal: one specific, confident, human message to one real person, proving the
applicant read *this* role and has a real reason to want *this* job — never a
templated blast.

---

## The tier gate — this is the most important rule

Outreach works **because it's genuine**. The energy that makes a cold note land — "the
overlap felt too perfect to leave to chance" — only exists when there's an actual
connection: a real passion for the domain, a near-exact experience match, a personal
hook to the product. Without that, the same note reads as forced and *hurts* the
candidacy.

So gate it by fit:

- **Tier-1 (strong, genuine fit)** — recommend outreach proactively, and draft it
  without being asked. This is where it's worth the user's time and most likely to
  work. If a scan score is available, tier-1 ≈ the top band; standalone, judge it: does
  the user have a real hook to this company/role/domain in `fact-pack.md`?
- **Tier-2 / Tier-3 (decent or weak fit, no genuine hook)** — **don't push it.** Offer
  only if the user asks, and be honest: without a real connection the note is weaker
  and a generic "I'd be great for this" cold email can read as spam. If they still want
  it, write the most honest version possible — never manufacture a passion or
  connection the user doesn't have. (The never-fabricate rule is absolute here too.)

When you finish a tier-1 RUN, offer outreach as the natural next step. When it's
tier-2/3, finish at the application and mention outreach is available if they have a
personal angle worth playing.

---

## Two methods: email preferred, LinkedIn as fallback

- **Email (preferred)** — highest hit rate, lands in a real inbox, carries a subject
  line and full message. Use whenever the address is findable and the user is willing
  to cold-email.
- **LinkedIn message (fallback)** — when the email can't be found/verified, or the user
  would rather not cold-email. A shorter note (no subject line), same voice and hooks,
  trimmed to LinkedIn-message length. Still personal, still tier-gated.

On a strong tier-1 role you can prepare **both** — email as the primary, a LinkedIn
variant ready in case the address bounces or the user prefers it.

---

## Step 1 — Find the right person

Use LinkedIn research (the Chrome extension, if available, or guide the user) to
identify a **real named human**, not a generic address:

- **Hiring manager** — the person the role most likely reports to (a design role → a
  Head/Director of Design; an eng role → an Eng Manager). Best for a "here's why I'm a
  fit" note.
- **Recruiter** — the recruiter on the posting or a talent partner at the company.
  Best for a "just applied, is this still active, would you flag my application" note.
- If both are findable, the **hiring manager** usually gets the stronger version of the
  note; a recruiter is the safer bet for "is this live" logistics.

Never invent a name or title. If you can't confidently identify a person, say so and
fall back to LinkedIn outreach to the most plausible contact, or skip.

## Step 2 — Get the email address

Email deduction is **probabilistic — be honest about that**:

- Find the company's email **format** — via hunter.io in the browser (the user's manual
  flow), or by inferring the pattern from any one known company address
  (`first@`, `first.last@`, `flast@`, `firstl@`). Apply that pattern to the person's
  name.
- **Flag the confidence.** Tell the user whether the address is verified (hunter.io
  confirmed) or deduced (pattern-inferred and may bounce). Never present a guessed
  address as confirmed. Suggest a verification pass if it matters.
- **Optional upgrade:** hunter.io's API (free tier covers a small monthly volume) can
  *verify* an address rather than guess — note this to power users who want a higher hit
  rate, but the browser/manual flow is the default and needs no key.

If the address can't be found or verified and the user isn't comfortable sending to a
deduced address, switch to the **LinkedIn** method.

## Step 3 — Write the note (in the user's voice)

Load `voice.md` and hold it for every line — and load `cover-letter-playbook.md` too:
the opener hierarchy and the **anti-staccato rule** (flowing sentences, not clipped
fragments-for-effect — the #1 machine-written tell) apply here as much as in a cover
letter. Ground every claim in `fact-pack.md`. The note must read like the user wrote
it on a good day, not like AI cold-email.

### The structure (adapt it — don't stamp it)

This is a decoded pattern that works, **not a fill-in-the-blanks template.** Shape it to
the individual user's voice, material, and what they have prepped. A user with a dry,
minimal voice should get a shorter, plainer note than a warm conversational one. If the
user's `voice.md` points away from any move below, follow the voice.

**Subject line (email).** The strongest subjects stack a few identity hooks that each
map to something the role wants, then close with earned confidence. A working formula:

> `[personal connection or passion] · [hard technical/experience match] · [way of
> working or soft skill that maps to the JD] … [a confident line that this role fits]`

The point isn't the exact punctuation — it's that the subject does three things fast:
signals a genuine hook, signals a hard-skill match, and conveys real (not boastful)
confidence. Pull the three hooks from the actual overlap between `fact-pack.md` and
this JD. If the user has only one genuine hook, use one — a true single hook beats three
stretched ones.

**Body — a tight arc, ~4–5 short paragraphs:**

1. **Open human.** Note that they applied and are reaching out directly; it's fine to
   name the slight discomfort of cold-emailing — it disarms and reads as honest. One or
   two sentences.
2. **The proof, specific.** The one or two strongest *genuine* overlaps — ideally one
   personal/passion hook and one hard experience match — each concrete and drawn from
   real history. This is the paragraph that does the work; it must be unfakeable
   specifics, not adjectives. A framing like "two things rarely line up this cleanly"
   can set it up, but only if the two things are real.
3. **Soft ask + proof link.** A low-pressure "if the fit looks as strong on your end,
   I'd love to connect," plus a portfolio/relevant link if they have one.
4. **Sign off** in their normal style.

Keep it short — a recruiter or HM skims on a phone. Every sentence earns its place.

### LinkedIn variant

Same hooks and voice, no subject line, trimmed to a few sentences (LinkedIn truncates
and people read it as a message, not a letter): a one-line who/why, the single
strongest genuine overlap, a soft connect ask. Warm, brief, direct.

## Step 4 — Deliver, never auto-send

Output the email (subject + body) and/or LinkedIn message as **copy-paste-ready chat
text**, clearly labeled, with the target person, their inferred email + a confidence
note, and which method you'd lead with. Optionally save the email as a file if the user
wants to keep it.

**Stop before send.** Cold outreach must always be the user pressing send on something
genuinely personal — never auto-sent. Auto-sent cold email is where deliverability
collapses and personalized outreach turns into spam, which is against the spirit of the
whole move. (If the user has Gmail connected and explicitly asks you to draft it into a
Gmail *draft* for them to review and send, that's fine — but the human sends.)

## Step 5 — Enrich the library

Per "The library is a living system," capture anything true that surfaced — a personal
hook the user named that wasn't in `fact-pack.md`, a company they're especially excited
about — using their `library_enrichment.mode`. Record only what they actually said.

---

## Why tier-gating and never-fabricate matter most here

Outreach is the highest-trust, highest-risk move in the kit: it goes straight to a
human, unmediated by a form. A great genuine note opens doors; a manufactured one (a
faked passion, an invented connection, a guessed address presented as real) burns a
contact and the user's name with them. So the bar is the same as everywhere, just
higher-stakes: only real hooks, only honest confidence about the email, only on roles
where the fit is genuine, and always the human's finger on send.
